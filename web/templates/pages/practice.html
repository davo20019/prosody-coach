Tips for Spanish speakers — common patterns to soften

Patterns to watch for

  • Monotone speech. Spanish often uses flatter pitch in English. Exaggerate pitch at first — higher on stressed words, lower at sentence ends.
  • Equal syllable length. Spanish gives equal time to each syllable; English does not. Stretch stressed syllables, rush through unstressed ones. COMfortable, not com-for-ta-ble.
  • Missing reductions. Unstressed vowels become "schwa" (uh): to → tuh, for → fer, can → cun.
  • No strategic pauses. Pause briefly before important information.
  • Harsh intonation. Falling pitch throughout can sound angry. Rise slightly on positive statements.

Practice sentences

  • "I THINK we should WAIT until TOMORROW." — pause after "think"
  • "That's INteresting! Tell me MORE about it."
  • "I NEver said she STOLE my MOney."
