Tips for Spanish speakers (CLI: prosody tips)

Common patterns to avoid

  • Monotone speech. Spanish often uses flatter pitch in English. Exaggerate pitch changes at first — go higher on stressed words, lower at sentence ends.
  • Equal syllable length. Spanish gives equal time to each syllable; English does not. Stretch stressed syllables, rush through unstressed ones. "COMfortable", not "com-for-ta-ble".
  • Missing reductions. Unstressed vowels become "schwa" (uh): "to" → "tuh", "for" → "fer", "can" → "cun".
  • No strategic pauses. Pause briefly before important information.
  • Harsh intonation. Falling pitch throughout can sound angry. Rise slightly on positive statements.

Practice sentences

  • "I THINK we should WAIT until TOMORROW." (stress caps; pause after "think")
  • "That's INteresting! Tell me MORE about it." (rise on "interesting", fall on "more")
  • "I NEver said she STOLE my MOney." (each word can be stressed for different meanings)
